## H19. Where an answer comes from, context or weights, is recoverable, and readable from the state before the answer

Rewritten 2026-11-11. Notation is the frame's (`hypotheses/README.md` §2). This is the provenance
member of the group "where the answer comes from" (H19-H22) and the instrument `H7.md` needs.

### 1. The estimator: three conditions

Each item comes with a source passage that contains the answer. It is asked three ways, at `M = 0`,
greedy:

- **with** the passage;
- **without** it;
- **with an unrelated** passage of the same form (taken from another item).

The answer's source follows from the pattern:

| with | without | unrelated | label |
|---|---|---|---|
| right | right | right | weights |
| right | wrong | wrong | context |
| right | wrong | right | confidence cue: the presence of a passage, not its content, helps (an infrastructure or format effect, not provenance) |
| wrong | — | — | not answered |

Without the passage, the fact class (`m0_pass_probe.py --m0-rank`, `--m0-samples`) splits "wrong"
further into "stored, not read" and "lost". An item that is right with the passage and "stored, not read"
without it is where the context unlocks what the weights hold, which is the case a skill stage should
learn to handle without the passage (`H7.md`).

### 2. Statement

Before the first answer token, the state after reading the prompt carries which source the answer will
come from: a small head on the state predicts the label of §1 better than a lexical control.

**Estimator.** Leave-one-out accuracy and F1 of a small head on per-head state statistics (the H21
feature) against a bag-of-words classifier on the same prompts; the labels come only from §1, never from
the wording of the item. The per-head readout lens (`state_readout_lens.py`) on the same items says which
heads carry it.

**Refuter.** The head does no better than bag of words (paired over items), or no better than chance.

Design constraint taken from H22: the two classes must differ in where the fact lives, not in how the
prompt is worded. The same template and passage form are used for both; only whether the model holds the
fact differs.

### 3. Measured

Nothing on this claim. Close to it:

- The item sets of `HZLK.md` §6 are split by design into context, question (weights) and two-hop items;
  the per-path counts after training are in `H7.md` §3. The split is by construction, not by the three
  conditions.
- Stage S2 of `HZLK.md` §6 lists the three conditions as a gate: the gap between real and unrelated
  passages should grow on context items after training. Not run.
- H21's head got worse when a correct passage was prepended (F1 0.829 → 0.524 on 40 items): a pooled
  state head does not pick up a passage's content by itself, which is why the label here comes from
  behaviour and the head is only asked to predict it.

**Status.** Proposed. The three-condition run is inference only and uses existing tools; the head needs the
labels it produces.

### 4. History

- 2026-07 written as a contamination detector for the runtime: after the fine-tune A1, held-out tasks with
  retrieval off, on and shuffled would show whether domain knowledge leaked into weights trained on
  reasoning traces only (thresholds 0.30 / 0.15 / 0.05 rubric points, to be recalibrated). Never run; A1
  was never made.
- 2026-10-21 the owner groups H19-H22 as the series that says what is stored where and how it is
  distributed, which training needs.
- 2026-11-11 restated: the three conditions as the estimator of the source; the claim moves to whether the
  state carries the source before the answer. The owner's aim for erased or unreadable regions, to tell
  where the model takes an answer from, is this claim.
