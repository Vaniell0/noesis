## H25. The state is a substrate a model can be trained to compute on

Rewritten 2026-11-11 from the record kept in this file since 2026-08-16. Notation is the
frame's (`hypotheses/README.md` §2). The toy cell `experiments/A0_state_probe/micro_wkv.py`
stores the transpose of the kernel's state (key × value); every statement below holds in both
layouts. Each number carries its file; a number without a file is reproducible from the named
function.

The claim is four statements. Each has an estimator and a refuter, and ends with its status.

| | Statement | Status |
|---|---|---|
| R | *register*: the state carries a quantity across steps where it is absent and combines it with later input | shown on the toy; partial on one real checkpoint |
| D | *delegation*: the recurrence's own terms do the combining | toy: fixed by algebra; real: not discriminated |
| A | *attractor*: under ticks the state settles, and training decides where | lemma proved; both outcomes measured on real models |
| C | *capacity*: how many facts a head keeps apart | measured: 1-2 per head |

### 1. Two lemmas

**Open-loop lemma.** Call a cell *open-loop* when its controls `(r, w, k, v, α)_t` are functions
of the step's input `x_t` and of `t`, never of the state. From `S_t = S_{t−1} A_t + v_t k̃_tᵀ`
with `S_0 = 0`,

    S_T = Σ_{t ≤ T} v_t k̃_tᵀ A_{t+1} ⋯ A_T,        o_T = S_T r_T,

and every factor depends on one input only. Information from two different times meets only
through the products `A_{t+1} ⋯ A_T` (decay and erase) and the final read `r_T`. Writes alone
superpose.

*Corollary 1 (additivity).* Let `a` arrive at the first step and `b` at the last, with blank
steps between. If the last read `r_T` and last decay `d_T` are constants and the erase gate is 0
at every step, then `A_t = diag(d_t)` and `o_T = f(a) + g(b)`. For independent, zero-mean,
symmetric `a` and `b`, `E[o_T · ab] = 0`, so for any weights and any linear readout `y`,
`R²(y, ab) = −E[y²] / Var(ab) ≤ 0`.

*Corollary 2 (silent steps).* A step with blank input has constant controls and maps
`S ↦ S A + C` with constant `A` and `C`. Followed by a free final read, any number of silent
steps reparametrises the function formed at the last informative step and leaves the set of
computable functions unchanged (for invertible `A`, the generic case).

Both are checked by `micro_wkv.py --verify`: [4] the mixed second difference
`y(a,b) − y(a,b′) − y(a′,b) + y(a′,b′)` of the frozen cell at gate 0 is 2.7e-15 (0.22 with the
learned gate); [5] the parity cell's logit equals `(m·k₀(x))(u·v₀(x)) + c` over all 256 patterns at
`n_extra` = 0, 2 and 8, to 7e-15.

With the erase gate at 0 an open-loop head is a time-varying linear system: a bank of
per-channel filters whose time constants the input sets. A fixed reservoir (echo-state network)
is the special case with constant operators and a trained readout.

The real model is not open-loop. Inside one token step, layer `l` computes its controls from the
output of layer `l − 1`, which read that layer's state; across ticks, the fed latent is the last
layer's output. Information from two times combined beyond read, decay and erase, and any gain
from silent time, has to come from that closure. A toy built on the open-loop cell cannot show
either.

**Tick lemma.** Stated and proved in the frame (§3): with frozen controls a tick is affine, `A`
has a real spectrum interlacing the retentions, `ρ(A) < 1` lies between the two largest
retentions, the fixed point `S* = v k̃ᵀ (I − A)⁻¹` has rank 1, and settling and forgetting run at
one rate. Exact for layer 0 under a constant feed; silent about the closed loop, where the
controls move with the state.

### 2. R — register

**Statement.** A quantity shown once is carried by the state across steps where it is absent,
combined with a later input, and decisions (which operation, when to stop) are read from the
state.
**Estimator.** Staged tasks in which an operand appears at one step only; held-out R² or
accuracy, with causal controls on the state (zeroed, reset, swapped between examples).
**Refuter.** The task is solved with the state zeroed or reset between the steps, or swapping
states does not move the decision to the donor's value.

Toy cell (`micro_wkv.py`, Adam, head size 8, operands in [−3, 3], OOD |a|, |b| ∈ [3, 5]):

- Staged multiplication, `a` at step 0 and `b` at the last of 4 steps (`StagedController`): id R²
  0.9996, OOD 0.9239. A first design showed both operands at every step; it reached 0.9989 and
  `a·b` was decodable from the state at held-out R² 1.0000 (1600/400 split), which says nothing
  about the state, since the controller could multiply.
- The same with the last read and decay frozen (`FrozenFinalReadoutController`): id 0.9998, OOD
  0.8361.
- Chained operations (`ChainedController`: a load, then two of {add, multiply} chosen per round,
  never shown again): id 0.9980; add→add 0.9960, add→mul 0.9987, mul→add 0.9977, mul→mul
  0.9992; OOD 0.58-0.60. Deeper chains not run.
- State-dependent stopping (`halting_chain.py`, `results/halting_chain.json`): one operand per
  round, stop once the running sum crosses a threshold. With oracle labels, exact stop 0.953 and
  within one round 0.998, against 0.466 with the state reset every round and 0.334 for the best
  counter-only policy. Zeroing the state before round 2 gives 0.20. Swapping states moves the stop
  to the donor's sum in 94.8% of the 2376 cases where the two oracles disagree (own prefix 2.1%).
  The value of the sum at the stop is weaker: R² 0.75 in range, −13.7 on operands in [0.5, 1.5].
  One seed. From outcome reward alone (`halting_rl.py`, price 0.01 per round): lr 5e-4 reaches
  0.764 against 0.758 counter-only (SPRT 0.782; donor-following 38% against 25%); lr 3e-3
  collapses into early stops; labels first and reward after keeps 0.779 → 0.773
  (`halting_rl_lr5e-4.json`, `halting_rl_lr3e-3.json`, `halting_rl_curriculum.json`).

Real models:

- External result (correspondence, 2026-08-16): G1h 7.2B with a frozen backbone and a one-layer
  adapter on the state, symbolic operations chained to depths 4, 8, 16 and 32, 48 of 48, with
  swap, replace and zero controls.
- G1i 2.9B after ThinkChain (`g1i_zlk_phase1_v3_step500`), 200 prompts of 4-bit XOR, held-out
  linear probe on the state at L12, L16, L20 (`experiments/rl/wkv_linear_probe.py`,
  `results/wkv_linear_probe_v3_step500.json`): the full integer −0.02; bits 0-3 −0.02, 0.31,
  0.11, 0.27.

Withdrawn as evidence that silent steps compute: parity with silent steps (`ParityController`,
8 bits shown at once, 51 of 256 patterns held out). The 2026-09-04 run gave held-out 0.882,
0.980, 1.000, 0.941, 0.980 at `n_extra` = 0, 1, 2, 4, 8 (`results/parity_pause_token_g1i_toy.json`)
and was read as silent time buying computation, after arXiv 2505.21024. By Corollary 2 the
function class is the same at every `n_extra`. Over seeds 0-7 (`train_parity_task(8, n_extra,
seed=s)`) held-out correct of 51 is 45, 50, 51, 51, 43, 50, 51, 44 at `n_extra` = 0 (mean 0.944)
and 51, 51, 51, 50, 50, 48, 50, 46 at `n_extra` = 2 (mean 0.973). The difference is optimisation;
seed 0 is its widest case.

**Status.** Shown on the open-loop toy, where the state is the only channel between steps.
Partial on one real checkpoint (3 of 4 bits).

### 3. D — delegation

**Statement.** The combining is done by the recurrence's own terms, not by an interpreter
downstream that reads operands out of the state: when the recurrence law is altered at
inference, the output follows the altered law.
**Estimator.** Alter one term at one layer and one tick; compare the shift of a decoded task
variable in the real forward pass with the shift predicted by recomputing that layer under the
altered law and holding everything downstream fixed.
**Refuter.** Where the probe reads the task variable, the real shift is uncorrelated with the
predicted one; or the agreement is uniform across layers (redundancy) rather than local.

*Toy.* In the open-loop cell the question is answered by the lemma: `a·b` from two times can form
only through the last read, the last decay, or the erase term. With the first two frozen, the
erase term is the only route, for every setting of the weights. The 2026-08-23 ablation (read
and decay frozen, erase gate forced to 0: R² 0.9998 → −0.081) is Corollary 1, not a property of
the trained solution. With read and decay free (`StagedController`) the gate-0 output is not
additive (mixed second difference 1.9-5.7 on untrained cells) and the product survives through
them: 0.9996 → 0.8364 at gate 0, 0.9516 at gate 1.

The column `ablation_a_gate_0_r2` (`muon_vs_adam_toy.py`, `train_task`) is the R² of the ablated
output, not a change in R². By Corollary 1 it equals `−E[y₀²] / Var(ab)`: minus the power of
the additive output that remains when the erase path is removed. Every solution loses the
product; solutions differ in what remains:

| optimizer | seeds | id R² | OOD R² (mean ± sd) | remainder `E[y₀²] / Var(ab)`: range (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adam | 10 | 0.9987-0.9999 | 0.758 ± 0.072 | 0.21-7.46 (2.67) |
| Muon, lr 0.005 | 5 | 0.9996-0.9998 | 0.817 ± 0.071 | −0.003-0.076 (0.008) |
| Muon, lr 0.02 | 10 | 0.9977-0.9994 | 0.776 ± 0.012 | 0.017-1.94 (0.037) |

(`results/muon_vs_adam_multiseed.json`, `results/muon_vs_adam_toy_10seed.json`; Muon on hidden
weights, AdamW elsewhere.) The earlier reading, that Muon's solutions barely depend on the erase
gate, is wrong: they depend on it entirely, as Adam's do. A large remainder means the solution
writes large terms that the erase path has to undo; a small one means it writes little besides
the product. The optimizers differ in the size of the solution, which `H26.md` takes up.

*Real checkpoint* (`experiments/rl/counterfactual_decay_probe.py`; G1i 2.9B ThinkChain `v3_step500`;
`w` set to −3.0, retention ≈ 0.951, at the final think tick; probe target xor_bit1; 180 held-out
prompts):

| altered layer | probed layers | probe held-out R² | corr(actual, predicted shift) | mean abs shift: actual / predicted / residual | file |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | 12, 16, 20 | 0.351 | 0.881 | 0.0424 / 0.0491 / 0.0067 | `results/counterfactual_decay_v3_step500.json` |
| 16 | 16, 20, 24 | 0.015 | 0.617 | 0.1017 / 0.1032 / 0.0041 | `results/counterfactual_decay_v3_step500_L16.json` |
| 20 | 20, 24, 28 | 0.068 | 0.259 | 0.0049 / 0.0058 / 0.0028 | `results/counterfactual_decay_v3_step500_L20.json` |

The reading registered for this sweep (uniform 0.85-0.90 favours redundancy, sharp variation
favours specific channels) does not apply. At L16 and L20 the probe does not read the task
variable, so the correlations there concern a direction without task meaning. At L12, where it
does, the downstream layers mostly follow the altered law (residual about 14% of the predicted
shift). One layer and one target: a graded answer that does not separate delegation from
redundancy. The probe runs only on a GPU backend (`experiments/rl/loader.py`, peft →
`training/rwkv-peft/rwkvt/operator/rwkvop.py`).

**Status.** Toy: fixed by algebra, so it no longer tests anything about training. Real model: not
discriminated. By the open-loop lemma the three places where information from two times can meet
in one head are read, decay and erase; the discriminating form alters each of them, with a target
decodable at every altered layer.

### 4. A — attractor

**Statement.** Proved part: the tick lemma. Hypothesised part: training can make the closed loop
keep a fixed point per prompt, with spread that survives the ticks; and a tick sets its own
operator, because `w`, `α` and `κ̂` are computed from the fed latent.
**Estimator.** Channel spread over ticks on a prompt set (`experiments/rl/latent_geometry_probe.py`):
cross-prompt cosine, effective rank, PC1 share, centred step size, settle tick.
**Refuter.** After training for it, all prompts collapse to one point by the tick at which the
step size settles.

Real models, 36 prompts, ticks 0-8:

- G1k and G1l 2.9B: one common attractor. Cross-prompt cosine 0.998 → 1.000 by tick 7-8, PC1
  share 1.0, settle tick 6 (`results/latent_geometry_g1k29_multistep/`,
  `results/latent_geometry_g1l29_multistep/`).
- G1i 2.9B: prompts move apart, cosine 0.996 → 0.883 over 8 ticks, still moving (tick-to-tick
  cosine 0.98-0.99) (`results/latent_geometry/g1i29.json`, `results/latent_geometry_g1i29_multistep/`).
- G1d 0.4B after S1 (KL, T = 2, M = 4; `kl_T2_k4`): a point per prompt, and the spread grows with
  ticks: effective rank 3.2 → 7.9, PC1 share 0.47 → 0.27, step 1.03 → 0.08. Free answers flat over
  M = 1..8. The base 0.4B peaks at effective rank 4.8 and falls to 4.35. The same S1 with one tick
  (`kl_T2`) erases: 10 → 0 right of 72 by M = 8
  (`results/s1_pilot_g1d04b/gate_kl_T2_k4/latent_geometry.md`).

*Reading.* Settling is forgetting: what earlier turns left in a head decays as `ρ(A)ᵗ` while the
loop settles, so ticks spend the memory of earlier turns unless that history is held exactly
elsewhere (`HZLK.md` §6, QKV-ROSA). A loop that reaches a per-prompt point at tick 1 (G1d
`kl_T2_k4`, flat answers over M) carries the prompt and computes nothing more. Computation over
ticks needs the fed latent to keep changing the controls, so that tick `t` applies a different
operator from tick `t − 1`; that is the closed loop the lemma does not cover.

Toy, optimised controls (`micro_wkv.py`):

- Random draws (`jacobian_spectral_sampling`, head size 8, 2000 draws): `ρ(A)` mean 0.9527, sd
  0.017, range 0.79-0.97; no complex eigenvalue, none above 1. Sweeping the erase gate from 0 to 1
  at fixed `(k, w)` moves `ρ` from 0.9565 to 0.9559. Median `|λ₂/λ₁|` 0.976. Stability and erase
  are nearly decoupled, as the lemma says: a rank-1 erase cannot move `ρ` past the retentions.
- Pseudo-inverse ceiling (`pseudo_inverse_ceiling`): controls optimised directly to pull a state
  drifted by 146.5% back to its origin reach 75.7% (T = 1 tick), 58.3% (T = 4), 55.8% (T = 8). No
  exact undo exists, since decay and erase discard information (`verify` [3]).
- The T = 8 operator past its horizon (`verify_power_iteration_prediction`): spectrum magnitudes
  0.9998, 0.9998, 0.9896, 0.9477, 0.9113, 0.9003, 0.5527, 0.2147; error 1.06 at t = 1 → 0.56 at
  t = 8, then 0.821 (16), 1.690 (32), 3.318 (64), 6.071 (128), 20.38 (512), 39.01 (1024), toward
  the fixed point at `‖S* − S₀‖ / ‖S₀‖ ≈ 252`. The residual is `Σ cᵢ λᵢᵗ` over the whole spectrum.
  A marker optimised for T ticks and looped past T leaves its target at the rate of its slowest
  mode: the low error at T is a pass, not an approach.
- The marker statistic `delta_cos_prev` rising from 0.64-0.68 to 0.97-0.98 within 8 repeated
  ticks is the same contraction and does not depend on the loss.

**Status.** Lemma proved. On real models both outcomes occur: one point for all prompts, one point
per prompt. No trained loop yet keeps moving with a purpose. Not measured: per-tick `w`, `α` and
the spectrum of `A` at the fed latent on a real model; per-channel retention distributions across
checkpoints (the reported `retention_range` 0.5452-1.0000 is identical on base, step 200 and step
500 because it is the clamp's range, `state_trajectory_probe.py`).

### 5. C — capacity

**Statement.** A head keeps a bounded number of facts apart, and this bound, not raw size, limits
what the state can hold for a computation.
**Estimator.** Readout lens (gradient of future log-probability with respect to the state,
projected on its singular directions, validated against ablation) for occupied and read
directions; held-out linear probe for separable key→value facts, n = 400
(`experiments/rl/state_readout_lens.py`, `experiments/rl/state_capacity_probe.py`).
**Refuter.** Separable facts per head grow with the number of facts stored.

Raw size: 40 heads × 64 × 64 × 32 layers ≈ 10.5 MB at bf16 for G1i 2.9B (the earlier text had 32 heads and 8 MB; n_embd 2560 / 64 = 40). Measured on G1d 0.4B and
G1i 2.9B (`results/state_readout_lens_g1d04b.json`, `results/state_readout_lens_g1i.json`,
`results/state_capacity_g1d04b_n400.json`, `results/state_capacity_g1i_n400.json`; also `_g1k`,
`_g1l`, `_p1step100`): 12-29 directions occupied per head, the output depends on about half;
1-2 key→value facts linearly separable, about 3 recalled at 8 stored pairs; raising stored pairs
from 2 to 8 raises occupancy, not separable facts. Lens against ablation: Spearman 0.92-0.98. On
G1i, 13-16 live directions of 64. Stable rank of the state 1.2-1.3 wherever measured; a near-rank-1
state is not information-poor (the toy reaches held-out decodability 1.0000 at stable rank ≈ 1).

Trained for once (P1 staged recall, `experiments/rl/train_staged_recall.py`,
`results/staged_recall_p1_step100_final.json`): gradient-read directions +10-17%, ablation-read
directions −40-60%, readable capacity 7/32 → 3/32. The answer was sharpened onto fewer directions
of the same mixed state; the facts were not separated. Fewer directions with more separable facts
is separation, fewer with fewer is sharpening; only the pair of numbers tells them apart.

Probe battery on `v3_step500` (merged weights via `experiments/rl/merge_lora.py`;
`experiments/_common/results/zlk_phase1_v3_step500/`), against base G1i: MLP-IPC rose at every
layer (L0 0.37 → 0.70, L4 4.17 → 6.08, L16 4.12 → 6.53, L27 4.97 → 8.55, L31 4.46 → 8.44); linear
IPC ≈ 0 (L16 0.253); σ₁ 21-24 against 15-21 at step 200. More nonlinearly decodable content, not
more separable facts.

**Status.** Measured. Why the bound sits far below the size of the state (the state is a matrix trained in context, and keys of distinct items overlap) is `H12.md`. Interference between stored facts is what bounds it; a training stage
improves capacity only if separable facts per head rise (`HZLK.md` §6).

### 6. The registered refuters

The three refuters in the frontmatter were written for a numerical task after training:

1. An MLP probe (2 layers, 256 hidden) on mathematical prompts shows no rise in nonlinear content.
   Not run on mathematical prompts; on ordinary text the battery above rose.
2. Accuracy on linear systems with out-of-range coefficients is not above chance. Not run. Design:
   2×2 and 3×3 integer systems with unique solutions in [−20, 20]; base, trained, and trained with
   the state zeroed at the answer.
3. Zeroing the L16 state at the answer does not lower accuracy. Not run on this task.

They wait on a model that answers through ticks of its own (`HZLK.md` §6). Prediction kept from
the earlier text, restated in `M`: on linear systems the frontier `acc(M)` is a step at the rank of
the system rather than a smooth curve. A tick adds at most one rank-1 write per head, so where the
step falls depends on how many heads a solution spreads one system over.

### 7. Division of labour with exact recall

The state's writes are approximate (bf16, about three significant digits) and decay. Exact recall
is a different kind of memory: a suffix automaton (RWKV-8 ROSA). In QKV-ROSA each position's
Q, K and V are quantised to n-bit codes, and the output is the payload at the latest end of the
longest suffix match of the query codes in the key history (`RWKV-v8/251024_rosaQKV_run.py`):
exact sequences, no decay, read by match. The state assembles; ROSA retrieves. Only the QKV form
can be queried from a tick, which has Q, K and V but emits no token.

| Regime | Mechanism | Precision | Output tokens |
|---|---|---|---|
| state trained to compute | rank-1 writes | bf16, ~3 digits | 0 |
| the same, larger model | more heads | higher | 0 |
| QKV-ROSA branch | exact suffix match | exact symbols | 0 |
| Transformer chain of thought | visible steps | per digit | grows with steps |

The state's normalised-key writes belong to the family of soft content addressing in NTM and DNC,
which are documented as fragile to train (seed variance, unstable convergence). The toy results
here come from staged supervision that forces the state to be used; they do not show that the
addressing escapes that fragility at scale. What is specific to this cell is the tick lemma: a
rank-1 erase cannot push the spectral radius past the retentions.
